# A logarithmic boundary for finding a coloring

Take a linear triple system on N points whose triples form four perfect matchings. A coloring is a binary word giving both colors to every triple. For a colorable system write d for the maximum dimension of an affine binary subspace contained entirely in its colorings and write k* = 5N/12 − d. For a given integer allowance k ≥ 0 the promise is that the input is uncolorable or that k* ≤ k. On promised inputs a decision must distinguish these cases. The algorithm actually returns a checked coloring on the latter. A returned coloring on any other input also proves satisfiability. The algorithm can signal that the YES promise fails without claiming unsatisfiability off promise.

**Residual estimate.** Fix a maximizing flat for the analysis only. From every triple select a pair that has opposite colors on the entire flat. Such a pair exists because the two affine coordinate differences on a triple avoid the origin in the binary plane. The selected graph is bipartite, with degree at most four and distinct edges by linearity. The number c of its components equals d: its pair equations define a satisfying flat containing the chosen maximizing flat and of dimension c. Each component with s points, e edges has nonnegative surplus q = 16(s−1) − 7e. This follows from e ≤ min(floor(s²/4),2s), with equality in q only for an isolate or K4,4. There are 4N/3 edges, so sum q = 16k*.

Call a component exposed to the algorithm when it is four-regular and has at most 14 points. Giving its two shores opposite colors satisfies every triple touching any of its points, since all four incident triples of each point already use a selected pair in that component. This assignment is an autarky. On every other component e ≤ 15(s−1)/7. For 2 ≤ s ≤ 7 use e ≤ floor(s²/4) ≤ 2(s−1). For an unsaturated component of size 8 through 14 use e ≤ 2s−1. For s ≥ 15 use e ≤ 2s. The same inequality holds for an isolate. Thus 7e ≤ 15q on all components that were not exposed.

**The reader.** Search for any partial coloring of at most 14 points satisfying every touched triple using the assigned points alone. Delete its touched triples and record the assignment. Repeat until none exists. Any surviving triple of an exposed component would admit its old shore assignment restricted to points that still occur. Every surviving row touching one of those points still contains both of its selected endpoints. This contradicts exhaustion, regardless of which earlier autarkies the reader chose. Thus every surviving row comes from the other components. In particular the residual has at most floor(240k*/7) triples. No flat or component certificate enters the algorithm.

For fixed bound 14 this search is polynomial. At a root give the point color zero by complement symmetry. When a touched triple has just one color so far, every valid extension must give the opposite color to at least one of its at most two remaining points. Try those choices, up to 14 assignments. If a support through the root exists, one branch follows it. After at most N successful overwrites, terminate. For R residual rows, reject as outside the YES promise if 7R>240k. Otherwise choose an anchor in each row and try the two ways to give one other point the opposite color. A set of such choices is consistent precisely when its pairs form a bipartite graph. Any satisfying coloring supplies at least one of the two ways per row. Use the recorded autarkies to extend a witness. The worst-case time including uncolorable instances is 2^floor(240k/7) times a polynomial in N. Alternatively run the residual search unconditionally for an exact solver with input-dependent cost. Autarky elimination is classical. [Kullmann](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0166-218X(00)00262-6)

**The ETH converse.** Use the source 3-CNF encoding and connected padding in the earlier release (Three_Actors_Proofs_and_Experiments.zip, next_flat/PROOF.md and frontier2_near/PROOF.md). They give a connected core of V=324m points for a formula of m nonempty clauses. A satisfying formula gives a flat of dimension 2V/9+1, with a permanently opposite pair in a redundant row. Attach t extremal 12-point blocks by exchanging the third point of that row with one in the padding. The crossed rows each retain their opposite pair. For NO inputs even deleting the redundant row leaves the core unsatisfiable. The resulting connected input has N=V+12t. On YES its k* is at most 5V/12−2V/9−1 = 63m−1, independently of t. The construction takes polynomial time in N. No padding assumption about an arbitrary received input is needed.

Let h(N) be a nonnegative computable allowance, with h(N)/log N tending to infinity; assume that testing h(N)≥a for an integer a takes polynomial time in N. If a polynomial-time algorithm separated all uncolorable systems from satisfiable systems with k*≤h(N), sparse 3-SAT would admit subexponential time. Starting with t=1, double until h(324m+12t)≥63m. For every fixed ε>0, any sufficiently large t of order 2^(εm) succeeds because h(N)/log N→∞. Consequently the first successful t is 2^o(m). The graph construction, allowance tests, and hypothetical algorithm take 2^o(m) time. The sparse form of the Exponential Time Hypothesis (ETH) excludes this. This is a subexponential-time conditional lower bound, distinct from polynomial-reduction NP-hardness. Examples include h(N) of order log N times log log N, or a positive power of N. The forward result is unconditional for any h(N)=O(log N). Also, directly taking k=63m and t=1 rules out 2^o(k) poly(N) uniformly under ETH. [Impagliazzo, Paturi and Zane](https://doi.org/10.1006/jcss.2001.1774)

**Implication for the seven-source question.** Consider the explicit version in which each source is one four-matching system and a viable source contributes its label (1 through 7) by bitwise OR to the three answers. On the promise that every viable source has gap at most a fixed constant times the logarithm of its own order, the source reader computes all seven viability bits in polynomial time and therefore supplies a false nonempty three-bit pattern. This also applies after any exact polynomial graph decoding that supplies these source systems. The new source-level test exhausts all 128 viable-source sets using 14 checked, equal-size positive and negative inputs. It does not independently rerun the three-color graph decoder on those inputs.

In the other direction, giving such a uniform exclusion for seven explicit independent sources under any allowance h growing faster than log as above would contradict ETH. In the usual pruning algorithm keep at most six prefixes, split them on the next variable, and take seven at a time. For each source restrict the original sparse formula by that prefix or append its literals as unit clauses. The number of clauses is O(m+n), O(n) on a sparse instance with n variables. Encode each one and pad it separately until its promise holds; as just shown each source takes 2^o(n) time and size. The true OR equals the unique viable label if exactly one prefix can extend. Excluding a false nonzero pattern lets us remove a prefix without losing all solutions. If several are viable any single deletion suffices, and for all-dead batches any syntactically valid label is harmless. A total polynomial-time actor gives at most polynomially many such subexponential queries. Verify the remaining full assignments to decide sparse 3-SAT in 2^o(n) time. The same implication holds for polynomial-size graph compilations preserving this source contract. This result depends on ETH. It offers no obstruction to P=NP in the absence of that hypothesis.

**Scope and tests.** The logarithmic boundary is for witness recovery or satisfiability on the stated two-sided promise. Recognizing whether an arbitrary colorable input meets a given flat threshold is a different problem. The exact-extremality algorithm in ../overlap addresses equality separately. The replay uses shuffled extremizers through 300 points, an uncolorable affine plane, a 33-point affirmative case leaving 44 rows, a connected 69-point mixture with the same residual, 96 independent small truth-table checks, and exhaustive partial-assignment checks at support four. ../stability proves an infinite family attaining the residual-size coefficient, and controls where one further global inspection succeeds even when local overwrites fail.
