# A sharp threshold for bounded-support NAE autarkies

Let H be a linear triple system on V points, given as four perfect matchings, so M=4V/3. A partial two-coloring is an NAE autarky if every triple touching its assigned points already contains two oppositely assigned points. Its restriction to any subset of the input triples is still an autarky after unused points are dropped. Deleting every touched triple preserves satisfiability, and the deleted assignments combine with any satisfying residual coloring. An affine flat here consists entirely of perfect colorings. All numbers below are exact. Priority of the combined results remains unassessed.

## The upper threshold and stability

A flat of dimension d yields a bipartite certificate graph G by choosing, in every triple, one pair that is identically opposite throughout the flat. Such a pair exists because the affine image of the two binary coordinate differences avoids (0,0). Write c for the number of connected components including isolated points. The d-dimensional flat lies inside the solution space of the chosen pair equations, so c≥d. G has M distinct edges and maximum degree four.

For a connected nontrivial component on s vertices with e edges use its defect

    q = 16(s−1) − 7e.

It is nonnegative by e≤min(floor(s²/4),2s). Its unique zero case is K4,4. Isolated points contribute zero. The sum of defects is 16(5V/12−c), at most 16δ for δ=5V/12−d.

**First stability bound.** Apart from K4,4 components and isolated points, the total number of vertices of G is at most 40δ. For a component with s≤8 check the two shore bound directly, using e≤15 when s=8 and it is not K4,4. At s=9 maximum degree four and the smaller shore size give e≤16. At s≥10, q≥2s−16≥2s/5. These yield s≤5q/2. Similarly e≤20(s−1)/9 outside the K4,4 case. For s≤9 use the same bounds, and for s≥10 use e≤2s. Consequently a flat of dimension strictly greater than 2V/5 implies that a K4,4 component exists. Its eight vertices use all four incidences in the selected pairs. Giving its shores opposite bits is an autarky. It is found by testing constant-size candidates directly on the triples.

The connected 15-point example has triples (A_i,B_(i+s),C_(i+2s)) for i in Z/5 and s=0,1,2,3. Each s gives one matching. Each bipartite shadow between two different letter groups is K5,5 minus a perfect matching. The A-B selected pairs give dimension six, and floor(5·15/12)=6 proves maximality. The ten non-isolated points attain 40δ at δ=1/4. The shadow contains no K4,4: the two shores cannot both meet the same letter group, hence one shore is entirely in a single group. Four points of that group have at most one common neighbor in each other group. This establishes sharpness of the K4,4 threshold, on an instance where other autarkies can exist.

**The bounded-support threshold.** If d>17V/45, H has an autarky on at most 14 points. There is a uniform polynomial-time reader for this promise. For every fixed h≥14, the constant 17/45 is sharp even on connected systems if the requested output is an autarky with at most h assigned points.

Call a component good if it is four-regular and s≤14. In every other component

    e ≤ 15(s−1)/7.

For 2≤s≤7 use floor(s²/4)≤2(s−1). For 8≤s≤14 an unsaturated component has e≤2s−1≤15(s−1)/7. For s≥15 use 2s≤15(s−1)/7. Isolated points obey the same inequality. If there are no good components, M≤15(V−c)/7, or c≤17V/45. A good component touches exactly its own selected rows: each of its points already spends all four hypergraph incidences on selected pairs in the component. Opposite shore colors therefore give an autarky. All connected assignments on at most 14 points can be checked in polynomial time by direct enumeration. The primal graph has degree at most eight, so for fixed size there are only O(V) connected candidates. A disconnected autarky splits into autarkies on the connected components of its domain in the primal graph.

**A sharp residual bound.** Iteratively find any nonempty autarky using at most 14 points and remove all touched triples. In the final residual, all rows originally selected by a good component have disappeared. Indeed, take that component's original shore coloring and restrict its domain to still-occurring points. If one of its selected rows survives, both of its selected endpoints still occur, and every surviving row meeting a still-occurring component point contains a selected pair from the component. This yields a residual autarky, contradicting termination. Thus all residual rows came from bad components. There q≥7e/15 by the last inequality. The number m of residual triples therefore satisfies

    m ≤ floor(240δ/7).

The high-girth construction below attains equality for every fixed support cap h≥14, with δ=7V/180. One can solve the remaining m clauses in 3^m times a polynomial by choosing one pair per triple and checking bipartiteness, then extending through recorded overwrites. In particular, near-extremal YES inputs with δ=O(log V) are tractable by this approach. For a decision promise that includes unrestricted NO instances, cap the residual search by the advertised threshold and reject excess residual size; this is sound under that promise.

## Tight obstruction for every fixed support bound

A component carrying K4,4 with one edge removed is called a core for this proof. It has eight points and 15 selected pairs. Each of its 15 triples consists of one of these pairs and a third point in a different atom. An atom is either a core or one additional single point. In every core the two endpoints of the missing edge each occur once as a third point for another core; all other core points are already saturated. Each single-point atom occurs in four third-point positions. The graph on atoms has one edge per triple between the core owning its pair and the atom holding its third point.

Here is a finite connected template. Take four cores indexed b=0,1,2,3 and 13 single-point atoms. In matching s, take pairs A_(b,i), B_(b,i XOR s XOR b) for i=0,1,2,3 except (b,i)=(s,0). The 15 third positions in that matching use the 13 singles and both missing-edge endpoints in core s. Give these two extra endpoints to pairs in a core different from s, and distribute the remaining positions arbitrarily so the atom graph is connected. For example the schedule in run.py is explicit. The template uses 45 physical points, and each matching partitions them. Its atom graph can have parallel edges.

Take a finite connected cover of this atom graph of girth g>max(h,14), preserving each labeled attachment. Such covers exist for any g. For a loopless finite multigraph with shortest cycle length k (two for a parallel pair), use the fiber of binary vectors indexed by its edges. Traversing edge e changes exactly its own coordinate. Every nonbacktracking closed walk of length at most k that could lift to a simple cycle would have to be a shortest simple base cycle. Its distinct edges have nonzero parity vector, so it cannot close in the cover. Every connected component of a covering of the connected template still covers the entire template with constant sheet number. Repeat to raise girth as needed. This elementary existence argument makes no efficient-size claim as g varies.

In the lifted instance every pair of points occurs in at most one triple. Within each core its 15 selected pairs are distinct. Cross-atom pairs can occur only in the unique triple associated with the unique edge between their atoms because girth exceeds two. Every matching remains perfect. Connectivity follows from connectivity of each core and of the atom graph. With t sheets there are V=45t physical points, 4t cores and 13t singles. A phase for each core, opposite on its shores, together with one independent bit per single gives a flat of dimension 17t=17V/45. Any third point may take its dictated bit since the core pair already works.

**Lower bound on every supported autarky.** The support of an NAE autarky is a stopping set: every touched triple contains at least two supported points. Consider any nonempty such set and its occupied atoms. A supported single needs an occupied neighbor for each of its four incident rows. Within an occupied core let U be the nonempty subset of its supported vertices. Each selected edge with exactly one endpoint in U needs its external third atom occupied. Each endpoint of the missing edge in U also needs an occupied external core in its own row as a third point. These requirements go to distinct neighbors since the atom graph is simple. If U is the full core, both missing-edge endpoints are present. If U is proper, its cut in the core has at least three edges: every nontrivial cut in K4,4 has at least four edges, as seen by considering the smaller vertex side of size 1–4 and bounding its internal edges by its two-shore product. Deleting one edge costs at most one. Thus every occupied atom has at least two distinct occupied neighbors. This finite induced subgraph contains a cycle. It has at least g occupied atoms, hence at least g supported physical points. No nonempty autarky of size at most h exists. By the upper implication there cannot be a flat larger than 17V/45, giving exact equality of the maximum on these instances. The initial m=M=4V/3 rows also equal (240/7)(5V/12−17V/45).

The term “stopping set” is established in sparse coding theory. Here it is a necessary property of the selected support; the proof derives the claimed lower bound directly. [Stopping sets in Tanner graphs](https://doi.org/10.1155/2010/432495)

## What the actors can still infer from the input

On the same constructed family the complete hidden flat can be read from unlabeled triples in polynomial time. Enumerate eight-point bipartite patterns with 4+4 shores and 15 distinct designated cross pairs, one per different triple. When g>8 each such K4,4-minus-edge pattern lies in one atom. Otherwise its at most eight occupied atoms induce a forest, and the connected pattern must use an edge joining two of them. Any such cut edge of the forest can occur in only one of the pattern's designated pairs: it belongs to only one triple. This would be a bridge of K4,4-minus-edge, impossible. Inside a core the only possible pattern consists of its 15 genuine selected pairs, and a single-point atom contains none. The bounded-degree primal graph permits linear-time enumeration up to a fixed pattern-dependent constant. Check that the discovered cores are point-disjoint and cover every triple by one designated pair. Assign their shores oppositely and the remaining points arbitrarily. On other inputs a failed certificate check yields abstention.

Thus the obstruction distinguishes strictly local overwrites from coordinated assignments. It is not a hardness barrier for raw-input graph computation. The finite experiment is stronger than an abstract construction test: on a separately built 67-sheet lift of girth five, the motif reader itself found all 268 cores, recovered a dimension-1,139 flat, and verified all 4,020 rows. The proof for arbitrary sizes uses g>8.

## Reproduction

Run python3 run.py and compare its JSON output with RESULTS.json. The script checks 29,885 feasible component profiles, the 15-point control, all 255 nonempty masks on a core, and a connected cyclic voltage lift with 3,015 physical points and 4,020 rows. All matchings, cross-pair uniqueness, affine rank, atom girth, and the output witness are checked directly. An independent support-closure enumeration with translation symmetry tests all possible stopping sets of size up to four on this girth-five lift. The arbitrarily high-girth statement used to prove the sharp thresholds is a mathematical construction.
