# Recognizing exact equality by covering the triples

Let H be a linear system of four perfect matchings into triples. Write V for the number of points and M=4V/3 for the number of triples. Equality here refers to a satisfying affine flat over the binary field of dimension exactly 5V/12. If this is not an integer equality is impossible. A grid consists of two disjoint shores of four points each. Every cross-shore pair occurs in its own triple and that triple has exactly two grid points. The sixteen distinct triples form its footprint. A footprint may have multiple grid realizations.

## Exact cover, counting and tractable cases

The counting inequality in the affine-flat proof can be tight in a connected component of selected opposite pairs only for an isolated vertex or for a complete bipartite graph K4,4. For a nontrivial component of s points equality requires e=16(s−1)/7<=min(floor(s²/4),2s); the two bounds give strict inequality at every s except eight (direct substitution below eight), and at s=8 equality requires the unique complete 4+4 bipartite graph. An active grid point consumes all four of its incident triples. Its third point in each row must be isolated in the full selected graph, since a nonisolated point would need to be a selected endpoint in all four of its triples.

It follows that a sharp flat determines an exact cover of all rows by grid footprints. Conversely, choose such a cover and one grid realizing each of its footprints. The active vertices are disjoint: any shared active vertex would put all four of its incident rows in both footprints. A third vertex in any chosen row is inactive in that grid, and cannot be active in another grid because the row would then be covered twice. Choose one independent bit per grid (the other shore takes its complement) and one per inactive vertex. There are M/16=V/12 grids and V−8(V/12)=V/3 inactive points. The resulting dimension is 5V/12. In this flat the third point in any row varies independently of its two active neighbors, so that row has exactly one identically opposite pair. Hence a sharp flat determines *all* selected pairs uniquely. Its dimension equals that of the space of assignments of these pairs, which contains it, so the two flats agree. Thus the number of sharp flats is the sum, over exact covers, of the product of the numbers of distinct grid realizations for their constituent footprints.

All grids can be listed from the input in polynomial time, with O(V) candidates in the bounded-degree model. The shadow degree is at most eight. For a chosen point on one shore, try four of its at most eight neighbors as the opposite shore, take their common neighbors, choose the other three points on the first shore, and check the sixteen row incidences. Canonical shore ordering prevents duplicate grid counts.

Two immediate polynomial cases follow. First, if every row occurs in at most two distinct footprints, a row gives a contradiction, a unit, or an exclusive-or of two footprint bits. Propagating colors and fixed values in the resulting graph finds an exact cover or certifies none; this also counts sharp flats by weighting each of the two orientations of every free component by the product of selected multiplicities. More generally, if b rows belong to at least three distinct footprints, branching on one support at each such row takes at most C^b times polynomial work for an absolute constant C (each row meets only boundedly many grids). Second, form the graph whose vertices are distinct footprints, adjacent on intersection. A stable set of M/16 footprints is equivalent to equality. If this conflict graph is claw-free, a maximum-cardinality stable set is computable in polynomial time. Claw-freeness itself is testable by enumerating quadruples. The replay uses direct enumeration for its small conflict graphs and does not implement Minty's full algorithm. [Minty (1980)](https://doi.org/10.1016/0095-8956(80)90074-X) and [Nakamura & Tamura (2001)](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jorsj/44/2/44_KJ00001202031/_article).

Every footprint graph forbids an induced star with five leaves. If two footprints intersect, their active pairs in a shared triple intersect in at least one point. Both footprints therefore contain all four rows of that point. Five disjoint leaves would occupy at least twenty distinct rows of the center's sixteen. The four-leaf possibility is attained by the axis-plane example below. Consequently the claw-free guarantee does not apply to the whole input class.

## A graph lift and a globally inconsistent candidate family

Given a simple cubic bipartite graph G together with a proper edge coloring by three colors, replace each edge e by two points e_0,e_1. Every vertex u of G yields four rows indexed by s in GF(2)^2. Choose the three edge labels by the three distinct nonzero linear functionals on s, in edge-color order. On one bipartition class complement all three labels. Rows with the same s form a perfect matching: each original edge has opposite clone indices at its two ends. Two original edges meet in at most one base vertex and any ordered pair of prescribed clone indices there occurs in exactly one row. The lifted system is linear. For a connected base its lifted row system is connected, since each pair of clones of one edge can be joined via a clone of an adjacent edge. The lift is always colorable by giving two entire edge-color classes opposite binary values.

The shadow is the two-independent-twin blowup of the line graph of G. Opposite shores in a grid therefore use disjoint original edge types, and each shore uses at least two types. Adjacent base edges have at most one common neighbor edge in the line graph: the third edge at their common vertex, since a triangle is impossible. Consequently the types in a shore are pairwise disjoint. An opposite edge must meet all of them and has only two ends. Each shore thus uses exactly two disjoint types and both clones of each type. The resulting four edges are a base square; conversely every base square lifts to a grid, whose footprint is exactly the four copies of each of its four vertices. Exact covers of the lifted instance are precisely spanning square factors of G. The square conflict graph is claw-free: two squares through a common vertex in a subcubic graph share an incident edge. Three pairwise disjoint squares thus cannot all meet the four vertices of a fourth. Exact equality on the whole lift subclass can in particular be recognized by the preceding claw-free procedure with just the raw matching system as input.

To obtain a globally false but everywhere locally plausible control, take two copies of K3,3, delete one edge in each, then reconnect the endpoints by two crossed edges in the same matching. Every vertex of each piece is in a square within the piece. No square crosses the cut: it would use both crossed edges and, within each piece, the deleted edge to close the square. Each piece has six vertices, so internal squares cannot cover it exactly. Its lift is a connected, colorable, 36-point system with 48 triples. Each of 16 rows belongs to two candidate footprints and each of the other 32 belongs to four. None of the ten footprints can be assembled into an exact cover of the entire system. The 24-point lift of the cube graph has three covers and the same absence of single-candidate rows. In an independent example using all 64 cells of a 4×4×4 array as triples, with the three axis lines touching each cell as its system points, four perfect matchings come from the coordinate xor. The candidate checker finds twelve footprints (coordinate planes) and three distinct covers. One coordinate plane meets four disjoint transverse planes.

Run python3 run.py and compare its JSON with RESULTS.json. The code checks witnesses against all original rows, relabels the connected 12-, 24-, 60-, and 300-point extremal examples, checks the exact counts on small examples, and independently compares the candidate footprints in the two cubic graph lifts with squares of the base graphs. These reductions and controls do not classify unrestricted recognition complexity. Priority of these particular constructions has not been established.

## Refinement of the full-three classification and exact counting

This section uses the rectangle and compression lemmas proved in the companion recognition argument. Consider a row-connected YES component after the footprint-subset deletion reaches its fixed point, in which every row still has three distinct options. The earlier argument gives, on each row, the three labeled moves determined by an ordering of its footprint labels. A move of type i changes only position i, using the physical point designated by the other two positions. Consecutive distinct move types commute to precisely the same ordered terminal row, and a sequence of identical move types collapses to one.

Consider one connected component of ordered rows under these moves. Let L_i consist of all labels appearing in position i in that component. Starting at any state, any desired member of L_i can be attained with a single type-i move (allowing a stay). Indeed, take a path to a state with this label. Commute all i-moves to the beginning and collapse them. The remaining moves preserve the label. Other positions are fixed during that first move. Successively set positions 1, 2 and 3 in this way: every triple of labels in L_1 × L_2 × L_3 occurs. These three sets must therefore be pairwise disjoint since an ordered row has distinct labels. The map from this ordered component onto raw rows is surjective by the path lifting argument; it is also injective since a given row can have at most one ordering consistent with the three disjoint sets.

For fixed position i, moving only in the other two directions sweeps the entire four-by-four grid of the footprint in position i. Its spokes on the two shores fix the corresponding labels, and two such moves reach every cell with its consistent ordering. Each label in L_i therefore indexes exactly sixteen rows. Write the number of rows as 16t. Each direction has t labels. All i-lines contain exactly four distinct states, so there are 4t such lines. For a fixed i-label F, the number of states of its footprint in a given i-line is independent of the line. To see this, carry an i-line along a j-move or k-move using the four rectangles. In the grid fixed by the remaining label, this sends four cells bijectively to four cells of the destination line. The corresponding cells are related by that same non-i move, hence they have the same i-label. Along an i-move the i-line itself is unchanged. Connectivity gives the asserted uniformity. As F contains sixteen states, each line meets it in exactly 16/(4t)=4/t states. Thus t divides four. The case t=1 would give sixteen raw rows, where every 16-row footprint is the entire set, contrary to three distinct options per row. **The only possible connected full-three YES sizes are 32 or 64 rows.**

For 64 rows there are four possible labels at each position, all 64 combinations occur, and hence each combination gives a distinct row. An i-line allows every coordinate-i label and leaves the two other coordinates fixed. Consequently the row-point incidence is isomorphic to the 4×4×4 array with the axis lines as points. The four given matchings provide a four-symbol labeling of the cells with each symbol occurring once per axis line. The 32-row size occurs as well: quotient the same cube over GF(4) by simultaneous addition of the same nonzero element to all three coordinates. The row orbits have size two, as do the axis-line orbits. Give an orbit of (x,y,z) matching label x+ωy+ω²z, invariant because 1+ω+ω²=0. Each line meets each matching once. Two distinct directions still meet in at most one row orbit, since the translation changes the third coordinate as well. Constant-coordinate planes and their translates pair up into six distinct 16-row footprints. Both quotient planes of any one direction partition all rows. The three directions give three covers and every row has three options, as the replay independently verifies.

The completeness of all label triples gives a direct counting consequence. In any exact footprint cover, let x_a,y_b,z_c be the zero-one selection indicators of the three coordinate classes. For every (a,b,c), the row condition is the integer equation x_a+y_b+z_c=1. Comparing equations that differ in only one position makes that position's indicator constant. Exactly one class is entirely selected. Thus every full-three YES component has exactly three footprint covers. All its footprints have unique grid realizations. For an alternative footprint the four sites of its inactive shore have exactly four eligible common active partners in this component. For an original footprint any second realization would be an alternative and coincide with the candidate footprint of an active endpoint in any covered row, forcing that row to have at most two distinct options, impossible. Therefore every such component has exactly three sharp flats.

In binary-option components multiply the numbers of grid realizations for all footprints selected under one solution of the unary and XOR constraints. Each connected parity-component has at most two solutions (pinning may leave one). Add their weighted multiplicities and multiply over independent components. Pruned candidates occur in no exact cover. Together with the full-three result (or the bounded explicit enumeration of its weighted covers), this gives a uniform deterministic polynomial-time algorithm for the exact number of flats of dimension 5V/12. Output zero if that dimension is not integral or the covering equations are infeasible. The integer bit length is polynomial: there are O(V) candidate grids, and distinct tilings yield distinct subsets of that list. This is counting on the exact equality slice. Counting all satisfying assignments of the input is a separate complexity problem.

## Independent comparison with an exact-cover oracle

Run python3 stress.py and compare with STRESS_RESULTS.json. On 197 inputs, a separately coded three-choice recognizer and exhaustive weighted exact cover agreed on both existence and the exact number of sharp flats (92 positive and 105 zero). Positive counts observed were 1, 2, 3, 9 and 27. The sample includes 128 two- and three-sheet cubical incidence lifts (gauge-only, planted, rare and general changes), all small cubic bipartite presentations with one matching fixed on 3×3 and 4×4 vertices, 50 additional seeded cubic lifts, the connected crossed-island control, four extremal cyclic fixtures and the 32-row quotient cube. The branch coverage includes initial zero, more than three, pruned-to-zero, homogeneous binary, and bounded all-three. No genuine mixed or oversized full-three post-pruning raw input was observed in this test corpus. The proofs establish the YES size limit and the forbidden mixing. The elementary XOR and finite bounds are distinct from claims about selecting an arbitrary Boolean witness.
