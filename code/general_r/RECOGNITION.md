# Recognition and counting for arbitrary regularity

**Corollary.** Given a linear 3-uniform r-regular hypergraph, r≥2, one can construct an all-satisfying affine flat of dimension (r+1)N/(3r) or certify that none exists, and compute the exact number of such flats, in deterministic time 2^O(r) times a polynomial in the input length. The four-matching recognition proof in ../overlap/RECOGNITION.md extends as follows. A resolution, if present, is optional. Equality refers to the exact rational bound.

**Footprints.** By the component inequality in PROOF.md, choosing an extremal flat is equivalent to choosing disjoint r-by-r grids of distinct triples covering all rows. Each grid comes from a complete bipartite K(r,r) selected in the point shadow using a different row per cross pair. Its footprint is those r² rows. All points of a witnessing grid occur at one cross pair in each of their r incident rows; hence disjoint footprints have disjoint witnessing point sets. To enumerate, fix a point p and choose one of two other vertices from each of its r rows, thus proposing the opposite shore. The common neighbors of that shore number at most 2r. Among them choose r−1 vertices to accompany p. Verify all r² pairs in distinct rows. De-duplicate footprints, retaining the number of distinct witnessing unordered bipartitions. The enumeration costs at most N·2^r·binom(2r,r)·poly(N).

**Relative rigidity.** Suppose some exact cover exists, and fix one for the proof. Every row consists of the endpoints a,b of one selected edge in an old grid F and one free point z. Free points are independent in the shadow; the shadow on old active vertices is exactly the union of their K(r,r)'s. Define U(a) as the set of r distinct free neighbors of a. An admissible new grid using only old active points must be an old grid. Otherwise all free members of its two shores lie on one side. If an active a also lay on that side, the opposite shore would be the r old opposite neighbors of a. A free z on the mixed side is joined to all r of them through its r distinct incident triples; the other r old endpoints in those triples are all distinct by linearity and belong to a's old shore. One equals a. The grid would use two pairs from that same triple. Therefore a new grid has one shore consisting of a free set U, and all its opposite vertices v have U(v)=U. Its footprint consists of all the rows through U. Distinct U yield distinct footprints because every row has one old free point. In particular (a,b,z) has at most three footprint options, namely F, the footprint of U(a), and that of U(b).

Reject frequencies above three or below one. If a row has option set strictly containing that of an adjacent row, delete globally every option in the difference. This preserves all exact covers. Iterate and reject on any empty set. At a surviving three-option row, rows through a share F and U(a), those through b share F and U(b), and those through z share U(a) and U(b). A neighbor with fewer than three options would trigger deletion. Thus any connected YES component containing such a row has frequency three throughout.

**Homogeneous bound.** At a homogeneous three-option row the points a,b,z carry the label pairs {F,U(a)}, {F,U(b)}, {U(a),U(b)}. Those pairs are independent of which incident row is used: this is immediate for the active points. For free z, each candidate group U containing z needs at least r distinct active neighbors there with free-neighbor set U. There are exactly 2r such neighbors in total, and the two distinct groups at a row partition them into r and r. Thus each group has exactly one choice of its r active neighbors. Distinct groups give distinct footprints. An alternative footprint cannot be old in this homogeneous case, since one of the active endpoints in its row would make two of its three candidate labels coincide.

Within any surviving labeled footprint, its r² rows are indexed by its two sets of r witnessing points. Its 2r spoke points carry pairs {H,q}, where H labels the footprint. At every cell the two other labels, from its two spokes, differ. Start at one row and assign its labels positions 1,2,3. A move of type i follows the unique point that preserves the other two labels, replacing the ith label and keeping its position. It has at most r destinations including the current row. Two equal-type moves compress to one because they follow the same point. To interchange successive distinct types, retain the third label H. In its grid the successive moves use perpendicular spokes: after the first, the second fixes H and the label from the *other* spoke at the intermediate cell. Going around the rectangle the other way reaches the same cell with the same ordered labels; uniqueness only uses the distinct labels at each cell. Sorting moves gives at most r³ reachable ordered rows, since three successive types suffice and stays are allowed. Every row in the connected component is reachable. Counting incidences gives at most 3r eligible footprints there. Conversely the rows (A(i,j),B(j,k),C(k,i)) for i,j,k modulo r, grouped by i+j+k modulo r, form an r³-row example attaining the bound. Each coordinate plane gives a footprint; at most three per row and exact covers by planes prove these are all footprints. Planes of different orientations intersect, so there are precisely three extremal flats. Each footprint has one witnessing grid, as its 2r spokes are precisely the points appearing r times among its rows (other points appear only once).

**Finishing.** In each connected component discard mixed frequency regimes or homogeneous three-option regimes larger than r³. With at most two options per row, the exact-one choices form XOR equations and unit pins. For each unpinned parity component, its two orientations contribute the sum of their products of chosen footprint multiplicities; a pin fixes the orientation. In the remaining all-three case enumerate at most 2^(3r) masks and add the same products for valid covers. Empty sums reject, while a valid mask gives a witnessing flat. Distinct tile sets yield distinct flats, since each row's free third coordinate is independent and its active endpoints form its unique permanently opposite pair. Isolated rows are included in ordinary row connectivity. The answer counts at most 3^(rN/3) choices and has polynomial bit length. This completes the stated bound, including for variable r.

Run recognize.py alongside ../overlap/run.py and compare its JSON to RECOGNITION_RESULTS.json. The replay enumerates the raw grids, resolves three-option or XOR components, and compares counts to independent exact-cover search on the footprints. It tests homogeneous r³ cubes, one-block or two-block positives, disjoint unions, and an exclusion control at each degree. For r=2 the negative control uses a girth-six-or-higher incidence construction on 24 points. At r=3,4,5 it exchanges two points in one matching of the cube. Three-colorability/ordinary Boolean satisfiability on an unrestricted compiler is a different target.
