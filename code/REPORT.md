# Which distinctions can a coloring algorithm safely discard?

**Research and replication report · November 23, 2026**

A system in this report consists of V points and four partitions of the points into triples. Two different triples share at most one point. A valid binary coloring gives both colors to every triple. An affine flat of dimension d describes 2ᵈ colorings by d independent binary parameters. Each parameter setting must give a valid coloring.

**Main result.** The earlier paper proved the sharp upper bound d ≤ 5V/12 and left recognition of equality open. There is a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm that decides exact attainment, produces an extremal flat if one exists, and counts the distinct extremal flats. Here equality is literal, without rounding. This result holds independently of the arithmetic-circuit preprint.

| Question | Finding |
|---|---|
| Can one find a coloring without being given a large flat? | Yes, in polynomial time if every satisfiable input is promised a flat within O(log V) dimensions of the maximum possible value. The promise includes all uncolorable inputs. |
| How far can that promise be weakened? | Under the Exponential Time Hypothesis, any efficiently testable allowance growing asymptotically faster than log V precludes a polynomial-time algorithm for that promise problem. |
| Does this decide unrestricted SAT? | A polynomial-time exclusion on every batch of independent SAT branches remains open. The same actor works on a specified high-dimension subclass. |

**Reproducibility.** Both prior archives were independently extracted and replayed in this workspace. All 18 suites in the three-actor package and all six in the weighted-coloring package reproduced. A separate exact-cover oracle also agreed on 197 new inputs, including 105 negatives. New checks exercise exact counting, hostile examples, witness recovery and formal substitution. The companion archive includes the two original packages, proofs and new tests. These are working-paper results reviewed within this investigation. Independent human review and bibliographic priority remain outstanding.

## How to recognize and count the maximum

For each triple in a satisfying flat some pair is permanently opposite. To see this, take the two pairwise differences with one point fixed as anchor. Their affine image in the binary plane avoids zero. Every such affine set has a nonzero linear functional that is identically one, which says exactly that some pair is opposite. Pick one such pair per triple. The selected graph is bipartite. Giving each of its connected components an independent phase produces a valid flat. Its component count bounds the dimension of the original flat.

A component with s points and e selected pairs has e ≤ 16(s−1)/7. This follows from its two shores and maximum degree four. Equality occurs only at an isolated point or a complete bipartite graph with shores of four points each. Summing over 4V/3 pairs gives 5V/12. At equality each eight-point block consumes all four occurrences of each of its points. Its 16 triples form its footprint. The original question is now whether the triples can be partitioned into such footprints. Each block gives one phase and each isolated point gives one free bit.

**Why the exact cover becomes tractable.** Fix any solution for the proof. Every triple has two block points and one free point. Every alternative block either gives an original block or has an entire shore of four free points. In the latter case its 16 triples are determined by those free points. Hence a triple has at most three distinct possible footprints. All can be listed from the triples in polynomial time. Whenever the remaining choices at one row form a proper subset of the choices at a neighboring row, eliminate the difference globally. On a connected YES component, either every row now has at most two choices, or every row has exactly three. The former gives pinned binary parity equations.

**The 64-row bound.** In the three-choice case each footprint is a 4 × 4 grid. The three points of a triple correspond to the three pairs of its footprint labels. Order the labels and move to a neighboring row by keeping two. Consecutive moves changing different positions can be interchanged by taking the opposite corner of their common grid rectangle. Consecutive moves changing the same position collapse to one. Thus every row in the connected component is reachable in three ordered moves, with at most four choices per move. There are at most 64 rows and 12 distinct footprints. Exhaust this constant-size case. A 64-row cube attains the bound.

Each accepted footprint can have more than one choice of shores. Weight it by that multiplicity when counting covers. Each extremal flat has a unique opposite pair per triple, because its third point is independently free. This proves that the weighted count gives exactly the number of distinct extremal flats. The working proofs are in overlap/RECOGNITION.md. For a fixed additive allowance k, enumerating the O(k) exceptional rows also recognizes near-maximum flats in V^O(k) time. The faster coloring guarantee on the next page answers a different question.

## Recovering a coloring without seeing the flat

Let k be a nonnegative integer. The input is promised either to be uncolorable or to have a satisfying flat of dimension at least 5V/12 − k. Only the triples and the allowance are supplied. The output is a coloring or a negative decision under the promise.

Remove any assignment on at most 14 points for which every touched triple already contains two oppositely assigned points. Such an assignment, called an autarky, can be combined with any valid coloring of the remaining triples. To find one, color a root zero. Whenever a touched triple still has one color, try giving the opposite color to each of its at most two unassigned points. Limit the depth to 14, repeat with the next root, and stop after all roots fail. Save each overwrite. [Autarky theory](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0166-218X(00)00262-6)

**Residual theorem.** When overwrites are exhausted, at most ⌊240k/7⌋ triples remain on every promised YES input. Fix a maximum flat for the analysis only. For each component of its selected-pair graph put q = 16(s−1) − 7e. The sum of q is at most 16k. A four-regular component of at most 14 points is an autarky. If one of its selected rows survived earlier removals, restricting its shore coloring to the remaining points would still give an autarky. Every other component satisfies e ≤ 15(s−1)/7, hence e ≤ 15q/7. Summing proves the residual bound, regardless of the overwrite order.

If the residual exceeds the bound, the promised YES case is impossible. Otherwise try two candidate opposite pairs per remaining triple, both through a fixed point of the triple. One always works in a valid coloring. Bipartiteness checks the chosen pairs. Extend a witness through the stored assignments and check the input. The running time is at most 2^⌊240k/7⌋ times a polynomial in V. This yields the polynomial guarantee for logarithmic k. On unpromised inputs an oversized residual only excludes the promised large flat.

**Where the logarithmic guarantee ends.** In the earlier 3-SAT reduction a formula with m clauses gives a connected core of 324m points. Joined extremal padding can enlarge V while preserving unsatisfiability and keeping the YES deficit below 63m. Given any efficiently testable allowance h(V) growing faster than log V, double the padding until h(V) ≥ 63m. This takes only 2^o(m) time and size. A polynomial-time solver on the widened promise would solve sparse 3-SAT in subexponential time, contradicting ETH. This is a conditional time bound. The detailed reduction, the seven-source extension and the allowance convention are in peeling/PROOF.md. [Sparsification lemma](https://doi.org/10.1006/jcss.2001.1774)

## Controls that distinguish discovery from guessing

| Test | Outcome |
|---|---|
| Connected 48-point decoy | Five locally admissible footprints. Only one cover exists, and one footprint belongs to no cover. The exact reader recovers the unique extremal flat. |
| 48-point cube | Exactly 12 candidate footprints and three extremal flats. Every one of the 64 rows initially has three choices. |
| Small independent count | Full degree-four checks on 60 sampled 12-point inputs using a separate enumeration gave 57 with zero extremal flats and three with three. |
| Shuffled 300-point extremizer | The witness reader removes every triple in two independently relabeled runs, starting with no flat. The earlier symbolic certificate gives 125 independent parameters. |
| Connected mixed instance | On 69 points with a certified flat of dimension at least 27, three overwrites leave 44 triples. The residual search returns a checked coloring. |
| Failure of all small overwrites | A 3,015-point instance has a 1,139-dimensional flat. Exhaustive support search excludes every autarky on at most 14 points. A reader using the eight-point subgraphs recovers a full flat and verifies all 4,020 original triples. |

For a single guaranteed small overwrite, the threshold is sharp. A flat of dimension strictly greater than 17V/45 guarantees an autarky on at most 14 points. A connected high-girth construction at equality rules out autarkies up to any prescribed constant support size. It still admits a polynomial reader detecting its internal structure. The lower bound applies to the size of an overwrite. See stability/PROOF.md for the proof and the thresholds for smaller budgets.

**More than four partitions.** For r ≥ 2 perfect matchings, the affine bound becomes (r+1)V/(3r). Equality forces selected components that are isolates or complete bipartite graphs with r points on each shore. The supplement constructs connected extremizers at V = 3rt for every t ≥ 2. At t = 1 equality is equivalent to a pair of orthogonal Latin squares of order r, giving exactly two exceptions, r = 2 and r = 6. The recognition and counting proof extends to r-regular linear triples in 2^O(r) times a polynomial in V, even without the partitions supplied. [Orthogonal Latin squares](https://doi.org/10.4153/CJM-1960-016-5)

## What the weighted proof adds

Grok correctly identified the evidentiary gap in the review it saw. The later weighted-coloring archive contains the exact projection in projection/ and the simpler one-variable-per-point version in counting/. The new border/ supplement checks the formal approximation step separately. None of the extremal or Boolean algorithms above uses Anthropic’s lower bound.

**Exact and approximate specialization.** The coloring polynomial sums one monomial per valid coloring, weighting each point colored one. The construction encodes a deterministic verifier for a directed spanning cycle, with unique logical wire values once the reference and input are fixed. Giving two private points per nine-point piece weight zero forces its private word to 001; pinning one reference port fixes the phase. Give each input port its edge indeterminate. Exactly one contribution survives per directed Hamiltonian cycle. This identity holds over every field.

Suppose an arithmetic circuit produces the coloring polynomial plus ε times an error whose coefficients are formal power series in ε. Applying the same weight substitution produces the Hamiltonian-cycle polynomial plus ε times the substituted error. Each input or constant gate is relabeled at no increase in gate count. Therefore Theorem 1.4 of Anthropic’s preprint, if upheld, transfers its superpolynomial arithmetic bound to formal approximation of the whole coloring polynomial. This proof is about coefficientwise formal approximation. Randomized numerical approximation and Boolean branch selection have different guarantees. [Theorem 1.4](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.vp-vnp-arithmetic-lower-bounds.pdf)

**External verification status.** Anthropic’s release includes the paper, formal Lean statements and proof. The repository describes the paper and proof as human-unreviewed at release. A separate public audit reports successful checker runs and separate statement checks under its own criteria and describes itself as unrefereed. This investigation reran the coloring archives. It did not rebuild the external Lean development. [Anthropic](https://github.com/anthropics/formal-math/tree/main/vp-vnp) · [External audit record](https://carlostoledo.co/vp-vnp/)

**Prior work.** Darmann, Döcker and Dorn already proved NP-completeness of bicoloring this exact four-matching class. Frankl, Gryaznov and Talebanfard previously studied affine dimension of satisfying sets. Briquel and Koiran established algebraic hardness for general nonaffine Boolean constraint languages in characteristic different from two. The features to check for priority are the sharp dimension and its recognition, the witness-recovery threshold, and the restricted projection in every characteristic. ## The unrestricted Boolean question and publication

In the seven-source construction a viable source is a partial SAT assignment that can still be completed. Its label is one of seven nonzero three-bit strings. The true pattern is the bitwise OR of all viable labels. The actor sees the compiled graph and its designated star. Returning one *false nonzero* pattern on every colorable input would suffice to decide SAT in polynomial time, provided the actor is deterministic and polynomial-time on all inputs. If only one label is viable, its label is the true pattern and survives. If several are viable, removing any one leaves a survivor. Keep at most six prefixes between variable splits and check the final assignments. This belongs to the established membership-comparability framework. [Ogihara](https://doi.org/10.1137/S0097539793258131)

The new reader resolves all seven explicit sources on the promise that each satisfiable source has logarithmic affine deficit. This applies wherever those source systems can be decoded in polynomial time. The ordinary independent-input compiler imposes no such promise. In that setting a rule giving one safe exclusion every time remains the open step.

**The Paskian connection.** In the earlier three-piece example, B and C produce the parts required by A. This rules out the pattern “B and C alone” provided the witnesses can agree on their shared variables. The new gluing proof specifies which disagreements can be forgotten. If U and W are certified spaces of boundary flips allowed by the two pieces and U+W spans the boundary, any pair of witnesses can be aligned. In general one must compare their possible boundary values modulo that sum. Just one residual bit can already encode SAT when only separate witnesses are supplied. These are mathematical conditions on the actor’s observations and available symmetries.

**Publication recommendation.** Lead the working paper with the sharp dimension and the exact recognition and counting algorithm. Present the logarithmic witness-recovery guarantee and its ETH converse as the second main result. Include the decoy and homogeneous-grid controls with both. The three-way gluing theorem fits the discussion of safe forgetting. Keep the formal-polynomial projection as a separately labeled companion so the principal claims remain independent of an external preprint. Deposit the proof text and tests alongside the paper, and seek a combinatorics or complexity-theory referee for the covering argument and padding reduction. The general P versus NP question remains open. [Clay Mathematics Institute](https://www.claymath.org/millennium/p-vs-np/)
