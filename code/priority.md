# Priority check and nearest comparators

- **Affine dimension.** Frankl, Gryaznov and Talebanfard define the largest affine dimension of a satisfying set and prove in Theorem 19 that for 2-CNF it equals the maximum dimension of a projection defined by signed identifications and pins. Their theorem treats 2-CNF. The present family imposes ternary not-all-equal constraints. The $5N/12$ result concerns flats inside the satisfying set; calling the formula an affine disperser would assert a bound for the other output as well. For example, fix any triple to 000: its entire codimension-three flat is rejected. [ITCS 2022 paper](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.ITCS.2022.72)
- **The same hypergraph class.** Darmann, Döcker and Dorn, version 3, establish NP-completeness of finding whether a linear 3-uniform hypergraph with four prescribed partition classes is bicolorable, and show that at most three partition classes always admit a bicoloring. This is a decision theorem. [Primary source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.03395)
- **Nearby extremal parameter.** The balanced upper chromatic number maximizes the number of names in a balanced rainbow-free vertex coloring. A class of a maximum affine flat instead corresponds to one component of a signed equality system, with no balance condition on component sizes and an opposite pair required per triple. [Definition and comparison](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12516)
- **Weighted constraint polynomials.** Briquel and Koiran's Theorem 8 already gives VNP-completeness under polynomial projections for any Boolean language containing a nonaffine relation, which includes ternary NAE. They use the same product of one vertex variable for each 1 in the assignment, and use zero weights to force Boolean zero. They fix characteristic different from two; the theorem does not impose linearity or a split into four perfect matchings. The scope under review is a coefficient-one projection respecting those structural restrictions in every characteristic. [Full text, §§2 and 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0902.2300)
- **Counting colorings.** Bordewich, Dyer and Karpinski's Theorem 6.1 establishes #P-completeness of hypergraph proper color counts for broad bounded-degree, minimum-edge-size classes. A hardness theorem on the encompassing class does not establish hardness on its resolvable regular linear subclass. [Primary paper, §6.1](https://algorithms.leeds.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/sites/117/2017/09/bdk08.pdf)

Searches covered affine dimension and projections of SAT solutions, signed and ordinary hypergraph colorings, rainbow-free colorings and upper chromatic numbers, resolvable linear triple systems, bounded-degree exact counting, and weighted Boolean constraint polynomials. The sources above did not establish the exact sharp affine ratio or the restricted all-field projection. This targeted search cannot exclude an equivalent result in other terminology. The component density proof is elementary. No claim of priority is made.
