# Every fixed additive tolerance has a polynomial algorithm

Let V be the number of points of a linear system of four perfect matchings. A perfect coloring is a binary assignment that gives both colors to every triple. For each fixed nonnegative rational k one can decide whether the perfect colorings contain an affine flat of dimension at least ceil(5V/12 − k), and construct one if it exists, in time f(k) V^{O(k+1)}. The parameterized guarantee proved here is XP. k may be fractional and the input is allowed to be disconnected. The exact case k=0 is already treated by the tile recognition theorem.

**Deficient systems.** The tile-partition algorithm in ../overlap/RECOGNITION.md works for any finite linear family of triples on points of degree at most four. A candidate K4,4 with its cross pairs in 16 distinct triples already uses all four rows at each of its eight selected points. Under any true partition of rows, every row has two selected active points having degree four, and one free point of degree at most four. The old active shadow is still the disjoint union of complete bipartite blocks. The mixed-shore contradiction holds because a free point of a candidate tile necessarily occurs in exactly four rows. When all rows of a component have three surviving options, every free point in any row is incident to two alternative candidate tiles, hence has degree four. This gives all eight neighbors used in the homogeneous proof and permits the entire rectangle argument. Empty residuals have a vacuous tile partition. The implementation returns one set of tiles in this more general setting.

**Budget for exceptional rows.** Pick one permanently opposite pair per original row of a qualifying flat. Include isolated points in the component count c of the resulting bipartite selected graph. The flat of these pair equations contains the original flat, so c ≥ 5V/12−k. Let Δ(C)=16(s−1)−7e for component order s and size e. Then Δ(C) ≥ 0 and their sum is 16(5V/12−c). On every component except a single isolated point or a K4,4, e ≤ 5Δ(C). For s=2,…,7 use the bipartite edge bound floor(s²/4); for s=8 use e≤15; for s=9 the smaller shore has size at most four, hence e≤16. For s≥10 use e≤2s and 2s≤5(2s−16). Therefore at most floor(80k) selected edges occur in exceptional components. Each such edge names exactly one original row. The coefficient 80 in this estimate comes from summing component slacks; no claim of algorithmic optimality is made.

**Reconstruction.** Enumerate every set S of at most floor(80k) rows; include choices only when the remaining row count is a multiple of 16. Remove S and apply the polynomial tile-partition algorithm to the remaining, possibly deficient system. Independently choose one of three pairs from each row in S. Verify that their graph is bipartite and that its union with the returned tile edges has enough components. Every original point incident to a deleted row has residual degree less than four, so cannot be selected in any remaining tile. Thus the graph on pairs from S is vertex-disjoint from the nontrivial good components, *whatever* tile cover the recognizer returns. The number of good components is determined by their row count, so a choice among multiple covers cannot lower the dimension. Searching the exceptional choices costs at most 3^{floor(80k)} per subset. The procedure is sound by verification. For completeness take exactly the rows of the qualifying graph's exceptional components; the remaining original K4,4 tiles cover the residual rows. Isolates and free third points of exceptional rows require no guessing. A point in an exceptional component can be the free third member of a row in an intact tile.

**Finite controls.** Run python3 run.py with ../overlap/run.py available. A connected 24-point crossing between two tiles has zero full footprints and a selected graph of rank 15, giving maximum affine dimension exactly nine. The exact and half-unit allowance reject, while allowance one constructs the flat. A disconnected 36-point system consisting of this example and a Latin block admits dimension 14, reconstructed from the combined input. An independently uncolorable nine-point affine plane tests a genuinely fractional threshold. A 16-row partial system with sixteen free points of degree one still admits a tile, while an isolated row and an empty residual illustrate rejection and vacuous acceptance respectively.
